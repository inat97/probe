Fridge temperature records for food businesses in the UK and Ireland

UK & Ireland guide · Figures as of October 2026

The short answer. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland chilled food must be kept at 8°C or below; the FSA recommends setting fridges at 5°C or below so the food stays under that limit. In Ireland, the FSAI's record sheet uses 0°C to 5°C for chilled food. Both regulators suggest checking at least once a day, and both expect you to write down what you did when a reading is wrong.

Why 5°C when the limit is 8°C?

The 8°C limit is about the food. A fridge's air temperature swings every time the door opens, and food near the door warms faster than food at the back. The FSA's advice is to set the equipment at 5°C or below precisely so that the food inside stays at 8°C or below. Some foods need colder storage than 8°C, so always follow the manufacturer's storage instructions on the label.

How often to check

The FSA says to check fridges and chilled display equipment at least once a day, starting with your opening checks (SFBB chilled storage). The FSAI's record book recommends at least once per day too, and notes that some businesses choose to check fridges more often and to record freezers as well (FSAI SC2). Its example sheet has AM and PM columns for each unit.

Once a day is the floor, not the target. Many small kitchens take a reading at opening and another at close: the morning reading tells you whether the fridge held overnight, the evening one whether it coped with a day of the door opening.

How to take a reading you can trust

  • Display or dial: the FSA accepts the equipment's own display, provided you regularly check that it is accurate with a fridge thermometer or probe.
  • Probe between packs: to check the equipment is working, the FSA suggests probing between packs of chilled food with a clean, disinfected probe.
  • Food temperature: the FSAI suggests measuring a "dummy" food such as pre-packed jelly or water, or the core or surface temperature of food in the fridge, washing and disinfecting the probe before and after a core reading.
  • Check the probe itself: the FSA's "Prove it" sheet describes a simple test: in iced water a digital probe should read between -1°C and 1°C, in boiling water between 99°C and 101°C. If it is outside that, replace it or return it to be calibrated. If the manufacturer gives no schedule, check it regularly — the FSA gives once a month as an example.

What a fridge log should show

At minimum, each line needs the date, the unit, the reading, and who took it. The column that matters most is the one next to a bad reading. The FSAI's sheet has a "Comments/Action" column for exactly this, with a worked example of a gauge being adjusted and the unit re-checked an hour later. A useful log looks something like:

When a reading is too warm

The FSA's chilled storage sheet sets out what to do if a fridge or display breaks down:

  • Use other equipment, or move the food to a cold area.
  • If you cannot do that, or you do not know how long the equipment has been broken, contact the environmental health team at your local council for advice.
  • If food that needs refrigeration has been out of chilled storage for more than four hours, throw it away.
  • Write down what went wrong and what you did about it in your diary.

It also suggests asking whether the equipment is right for the job if problems keep recurring — commercial equipment is generally more suitable for catering.

The four-hour exception for display

For cold food on display — a buffet, for example — the FSA describes a "4 hour rule": food can be out of chilled storage for up to four hours, once only. Food displayed for less than four hours can go back in the fridge at 8°C or below until used; after four hours it must be thrown away. The FSA suggests labelling food with the time it came out, or noting it in your diary (SFBB chilled storage).

How long to keep the log

The FSA's SFBB page says completed diary pages should be kept safely until your next visit from a local authority food safety officer (GOV.UK: SFBB). If you are unsure how long your council or the FSAI expects you to keep records, ask them directly — and keep a copy somewhere other than the kitchen.

Where Probe fits

Probe keeps a temperature log for each of your units against targets you set. Its starting targets are 8°C or below for fridges, -18°C or below for freezers and 63°C or above for hot holding, and you can change any of them, including a lower limit — for example 5°C if that is what you work to, or 0°C to 5°C in Ireland. A reading outside its target cannot be saved until you write what you did about it, and the Insights screen ranks which unit has been out of range most often. Probe is not connected to any probe or sensor: you type the reading in.
