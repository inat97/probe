2. What Probe does

Probe lets you record opening and closing checks, temperature readings against target ranges you set, the action you took when a reading was outside target, free text diary entries, and a daily sign-off; see how complete your records are; and export a period as a PDF pack or a CSV file.

3. What the numbers are, and are not

Every figure Probe shows is arithmetic on what you typed. Probe does not measure temperature, is not connected to any probe, sensor or fridge, and cannot tell whether a check was actually carried out. A record in Probe is evidence that something was typed into a phone, at the time the app recorded it being typed. Nothing more is claimed.

The completeness figures — days signed off in full, days partly recorded, days with nothing, readings outside target, readings with no action written, readings entered after the day they belong to — are counts of your own entries against the fixed rules described in the app. They are a prompt to keep better records. They are not an audit, not a compliance score, and not a prediction of how any inspector will treat your book.

4. Target ranges are yours

Probe ships default target ranges so that it is usable on the first day. They are starting points chosen from figures commonly written into UK and EU rules, and they are editable. They are not advice, they may be wrong for your country, your equipment or the food you handle, and they can change. Check what applies to you and set your own.

5. Record keeping stays yours

What a food business must record, and for how long, differs by country and by circumstance. Probe cannot guarantee that records kept in it satisfy any particular requirement. Keep your own copies: export regularly and store the files somewhere other than the phone.
